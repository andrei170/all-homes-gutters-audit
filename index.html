Finding 1 — You Run Facebook Ads And There Is No Meta Pixel
What is installed: Google Analytics 4 G-5N37RZ1CMQ present Meta pixel not found Google Tag Manager not found (nowhere for one to hide) Google Ads conversion not found Call tracking not found Only Facebook reference on the site: facebook.com/allhomesseamlessgutters a link to your page
We checked this harder than usual, because your site is JavaScript-rendered

A Next.js site can load tags after the page renders, so a single check is not enough. We also read three of your compiled JavaScript bundles — the layout, the page and the main app chunk — and found no fbq, no connect.facebook.net and no pixel reference in any of them. Your GA4 tag is served in the HTML; nothing Meta-related is served anywhere alongside it.

What that costs you, while you are spending money on Facebook:

  • You cannot tell which ads produced work. Meta reports clicks and it cannot connect a click to a booked job.
  • You cannot retarget. Everybody who has clicked an ad, looked at your pricing page and not called is the cheapest audience you will ever have access to. None of it has been collected, and it cannot be recovered backwards.
  • Meta cannot optimise. Its delivery learns from conversions it can see. With no pixel it can only chase clicks, so you are paying for traffic and hoping.

This is a tag, not a redesign. It changes nothing a visitor sees and it is the single most expensive gap in the business right now.

A static read cannot prove absence with total certainty. Whoever runs your ads can confirm it in about a minute — and that is worth doing before your next invoice.

Finding 2 — Winter Is Not Your Off-Season. It Is The Season You Have Not Built For.
From your own structured data, your declared services include: Seamless gutter installation page exists Gutter guard installation page exists Gutter repair and maintenance page exists Fascia and soffit repair page exists Downspout installation page exists Commercial gutters page exists Ice dam prevention NO PAGE
You already know it matters. You have just never written it down.

Your gutter guard page mentions freezing nine times and ice dams four times. One of your own published reviews, from a customer in Geneva, reads: “Two winters in, no ice dams, no overflow.”

So the message exists, scattered across pages about other things, and has no home of its own. Somebody in Ashtabula County searching for help with ice dams in January finds nothing of yours, because you have not published a page for the search.

And the timing is the point. It is late September. Leaf fall is weeks away, which is gutter guard season. Behind that is lake-effect snow, which is ice dam season. The next four months contain two distinct products you already sell. Treating that as the slow period is a decision, not a fact about the weather.

Finding 2b — Your Town Pages Are Real. We Checked, Because Usually They Are Not.
This is the opposite of what we normally find

Forty-eight town pages is the pattern that is almost always faked — one page duplicated with the town name swapped, which Google treats as doorway pages and can penalise. So we read four of yours and compared them word by word.

Ashtabula 4,010 words Geneva 3,892 words Conneaut 3,921 words Steubenville 3,567 words Shared vocabulary, neighbouring towns: 90% Shared vocabulary, Ashtabula vs Steubenville: 79% Words unique to the Geneva page: geneva-on-the-lake · cottages · cellars · cork · farmhouses · erie Words unique to the Steubenville page: fort · historic · eastern · freeze-thaw · decades

Somebody actually wrote these. The Geneva page knows about Geneva-on-the-Lake, the cottages and the wine cellars. The Steubenville page knows about Fort Steuben and the older housing stock. The further apart the towns, the more the content diverges — which is exactly what genuine local writing looks like and exactly what a template does not do.

That is close to four thousand words per town, across forty-eight towns. It is the single most substantial piece of work on this website and it is better than anything else we have audited this month.

Which is why the missing pixel is so frustrating. Somebody built all of this and nobody can tell you which of the forty-eight towns produced a job.

Finding 2c — Nine Months, One Article

Two sprints and a long silence, and the silence is winter

Ten of your fifteen articles were published in two bursts, four days in June and six days in August. That is a content sprint, not a habit — and there is nothing wrong with that, it is how most of this gets done.

But between September 2025 and June 2026 you published one article. Those nine months contain the whole of leaf season and the whole of winter — the exact period you have just told us is slowing down.

The pattern is the point. The site goes quiet precisely when the work goes quiet, which means nothing is pulling in the searches that happen in November, December and January. And your existing articles are all installation, drainage, sizing and cost — none of them is about ice, snow or winter at all.

Finding 3 — Five Star Rating. Seven Reviews.
Your AggregateRating markup: 5.0 from 7 reviews Your job volume, as you told Josh: ~5 per week Which is roughly: 250+ jobs a year
The machinery is built. There is nothing in it.

You have AggregateRating structured data installed, which is what allows a star rating to appear beside your result in search. That is a real technical advantage and most contractors do not have it.

It is currently pointed at seven reviews. A homeowner comparing three gutter companies reads the review count before the rating — five stars from seven reads as a new business, and you are not one.

You have done hundreds of jobs and asked almost none of them. This costs nothing, needs no agency, and it is the highest-return thing on this page that you can start today.

We could not retrieve your Google Business Profile review count, so we do not know how many reviews you hold there. If it is substantially higher than seven, the fix is simply to reflect that on the site.

If you would turn down a job in Steubenville, that page is attracting enquiries you do not want and costing you the time to answer them. If you would take it, ignore this entirely.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Install and verify the Meta pixel, this week. Before another pound goes into Facebook. Everything else compounds; this one is losing money every day it is not there.
  • Build the ice dam prevention page — the service your own schema already claims. Then a gutter guard page angled at leaf fall, which is the next six weeks.
  • Start asking for reviews after every job. You have the markup; you need the reviews. This is free and it is the fastest-moving number on the page.
  • Add Google Ads conversion tracking and call tracking, so the phone calls count alongside form fills. In this trade most enquiries are calls.
  • Decide the real service area, and keep the pages for the towns you actually want.
  • Then run the winter campaign — leaf fall now, ice dams from November, with a pixel underneath it so the audience is still there next year.
